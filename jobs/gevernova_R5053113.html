General Context

  • The main objective of this partnership is to drastically reduce the mental workload of both the project teams and site supervisors during the complex construction and commissioning phases of power plants, allowing them to refocus on on-site execution and problem-solving.
  • The industry is facing increasingly extended project schedules , with civil engineering phases now lasting more than 12 months (compared with 9 to 10 months previously). This is further compounded by increasingly stringent safety standards, the growing scale of equipment, and a general erosion of hands-on technical expertise in the field.

Problem Statement

  • Document retrieval and mental workload: Teams are spending increasing amounts of time searching for documents and identifying errors as early as possible, at the expense of technical problem-solving. Typical examples include retrieving the latest approved version of a drawing, the RAM applicable to a specific task, lessons learned and archived “Boxes” from previous projects, or an up-to-date schedule extract (the overall schedule being cumbersome to manage and updated only once a month).
  • Fragmentation of technical data: Information is spread across a multitude of software tools and databases with complex architectures. The same data is not accessible to everyone, and exchanges with subcontractors and external partners add further complexity.
  • Technical change management: Engineering teams continuously update plans and documents in parallel with on-site execution. Beyond the challenge of ensuring that the documents used on site correspond to the latest approved revisions, the underlying challenge is the continuous management of these changes and the link between engineering and the site. Access rights further complicate the situation: they vary depending on the software used to access the documents, meaning that teams may not be able to access the same version of a document depending on the tool they use.
  • Heterogeneous documentation: Supplier-specific documentation does not have the same level of standardization as internal engineering documentation, making automated processing more difficult. Documents provided by contractors can be particularly complex to analyze, especially those originating from certain local contractors.
  • Procurement tracking and associated logistics: Maintaining the link between engineering, suppliers, and the site is challenging. Suppliers sometimes package items inconsistently with the assembly sequence, leading to apparent “shortages” where parts listed in the Bill of Materials (BOM) are physically on site but buried in unopened crates. Comparing the BOM with supplier packing lists remains a manual task. These discrepancies, when discovered late, result in costly delays.
  • Time-consuming reporting and written data entry: Reports (Excel files) and handwritten entries contribute to a decline in motivation. However, this reporting is valuable for future power plants: it is essential to make the most of field experience and lessons learned.

Identified Topics

  1. COLLECTION OF CRITICAL DOCUMENTS FOR DECISION-MAKING, RISK QUANTIFICATION AND ANTICIPATION (“PREPARATION BOX”)

Site challenges: Document retrieval and mental workload; fragmentation of technical data; technical change management.

Objective: Gather all documents required to support a decision, from different tools, into a single repository: a BOX , ensuring that the latest versions are used, with risk quantification and automatic alerts.

The BOX will operate iteratively, with completeness indicators. The main use case is preparation for the monographic meeting with the subcontractor , held approximately two weeks before work starts on an MLI (mechanical/electrical system or subsystem), based on the documentation available three to four weeks before the start of the work.

  1. SEMANTIC ANALYSIS OF “DELTAS” BETWEEN DOCUMENTS

Site challenges: Engineering change management; heterogeneous documentation.

A key point identified during discussions is that experienced engineers work by specifically identifying the “deltas” compared with previous projects.

Objective: Replicate this expert approach by automatically detecting discrepancies between plans and procedures for the current project and those from previous projects, as well as between successive revisions of the same document. The aim is to highlight hidden pitfalls before the execution phase.

Different versions of the same document may coexist in PLM and PPDM due to access restrictions. The module will identify these gaps and their causes and highlight the differences, allowing the expert to focus only on reviewing the deltas and assessing whether they have an impact on erection activities.

  1. SITE PROCUREMENT MANAGEMENT AND MATERIAL COMPLETENESS VERIFICATION (BOM / STOCK)

Site challenges: Procurement tracking and associated logistics.

Objective: Cross-check the theoretical Bill of Materials (BOM) — which continuously evolves as a result of changes in the field and therefore needs to be monitored continuously — against the actual situation on site.

The solution should detect real or apparent shortages (items not delivered vs. items already on site but not yet inventoried) before work starts, including partial supplier shipments, and predict whether tasks are feasible from a material availability perspective, thereby avoiding mobilizing teams for work that cannot be completed.

  1. C&C CONVERSATIONAL AI ASSISTANT

Site challenges: Document retrieval and mental workload; fragmentation of technical data.

Objective: Enable supervisors and MLI group leaders to directly query documentary sources and project schedules using natural language, without having to navigate through the underlying tools and systems.

  1. VOICE REPORTING AND LESSONS LEARNED CAPTURE

Site challenges: Time-consuming reporting and written contributions.

Objective: Replace tedious manual report entry with voice-based interfaces and capture field experience as effectively as possible for the benefit of future power plants.
