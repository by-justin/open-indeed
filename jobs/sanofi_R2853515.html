The mission — what this role is really here to do

This role exists to help a manufacturing accelerator become a learning system — one that improves faster than its constraints. That means building Lean and Kata habits into how teams, product owners, and leaders actually work. Not running workshops at them.

You’ll be operating inside a GxP-regulated, compliance-heavy environment. Governance is real here. The job isn’t to work around it — it’s to help the system flow better within it. That requires patience, credibility at the Gemba, and a coach who understands that training is not change.

Delivery discipline and product thinking — both matter. Most of our products are already in production. More Products are going live. Some teams are still building delivery muscle. Others are ready to move toward outcome thinking and genuine product ownership. The coach works at both levels simultaneously, calibrated to where each team actually is.

Before you apply — honest questions

If most of these don’t resonate, this probably isn’t the right role:

→ Do you prefer principles over practices — even cool ones?

→ Do you go to the Gemba first, and draw conclusions second?

→ Can you hold a credible conversation with a plant director, a data scientist, and a PO — in the same afternoon?

→ Would you rather build someone else’s capability than demonstrate your own?

→ Do you see a compliance constraint as a system condition to understand, not an obstacle to fight?

→ Have you ever coached in an environment where a 6-month change cycle is considered fast?

Main responsibilities

  • Flow first. Make the delivery system visible — flow boards, WIP policies, aging WIP reviews. You cannot improve what you cannot see.
  • Delivery discipline. Reduce batch size and increase delivery frequency. Small, releasable increments over large, risky releases.
  • Lean as thinking. Bring Lean thinking to the work: value stream mapping, constraint identification, waste removal — as habit, not training event.
  • Kata cycles. Run Kata coaching cycles with teams and leaders: current condition → target condition → experiment → learn. Repeat until it is instinct.
  • Gemba-first. Go to the Gemba. Observe real work. Build your interventions from what you see — not from what people report in standups.
  • Leadership coaching. Help leadership adopt a go-see, ask-why, show-respect posture .
  • GxP navigation. Navigate the GxP compliance environment intelligently. Change control is the operating context — design within it, not around it.
  • Product thinking. Coach product owners along the ownership arc: from backlog manager to problem owner. Outcome framing, evidence-based bets, assumption testing. For teams already in production, this work starts now. For teams still building delivery muscle, you plant the seeds.
  • System of coaches. Build coaching capability in others. Your job is to make yourself progressively less necessary.

About you

Experience

  • 7+ years coaching delivery teams — in the work, not above it.
  • Demonstrated flow metrics practice: cycle time, throughput, WIP as daily steering tools — not reporting.
  • Continuous delivery awareness and applied practice: batch size reduction, faster feedback loops, quality built in — hands-on exposure, not deep coaching expertise.
  • Lean and Kata as lived practice: VSM, A3, PDCA in real engagements. You know the difference between a root cause and a symptom.
  • Product ownership coaching experience: helping POs move from output delivery to outcome framing. Discovery practices are a plus — coaching toward evidence-based decisions is a requirement.
  • Regulated or compliance-heavy industry experience preferred. Not a hard filter — but no regulated experience means a steeper ramp.
  • Ability to guide teams on tool selection and integration without being tool-driven.

What we’re really looking for

  • Framework Agnostic. . Uses what works. Can explain why. No loyalty to a certification stack or a vendor coach .
  • Sports coach. Sports coaching posture: skin in the game, deliberate practice, willing to get operational if the team needs it.
  • Flow depth. Flow literacy deep enough to transfer to others — not just use personally. Story points are not a health metric.
  • Evidence-based. Evidence-based practitioner: current condition always anchored in data. Training is not change.
  • Product lens. Product thinking as a coaching lens. Can hold the outcome question alongside the flow question. Knows that features shipped without evidence of value is just waste with a velocity number on it.
  • Range. Communicates credibly across all levels — engineers, data scientists, POs, plant directors, senior leadership.
  • Learning posture. Curious and continuously learning. Active in communities, reads widely, experiments openly.
  • Language. Fluent English required. French is a genuine advantage for crucial conversations at the Gemba.

Hard filters — please don’t apply if…

  • You think running a workshop is the same as changing a system.
  • You measure progress in certifications rather than outcomes.
  • You’ve spent the last 5 years in a CoE with no sustained team contact.
  • Your CV is a list of activities — 'accompanied,' 'facilitated,' 'implemented' — with no before/after and no measurable system change. Activity is not evidence.
  • You can coach flow or product — but not both, and not simultaneously.
  • Your instinct when a system is struggling is to add a ceremony, not remove a constraint.
